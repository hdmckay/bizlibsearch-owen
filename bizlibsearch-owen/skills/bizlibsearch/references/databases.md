## 1. Affiliation & Access Matrix

| User type | What they get |
|---|---|
| Owen Grad School (student/faculty/staff) | Everything, including Owen-restricted resources and full Walker Management Library access |
| VU undergrad (incl. Hoogland Undergraduate Business Program) | Nearly all databases, remotely over VPN. Bloomberg terminals available at Calhoun Hall 2nd floor (UBP office) and 4th floor Central Library |
| VU faculty / staff (non-Owen) | Nearly all databases; standard VUnetID proxy access off-campus |
| Alumni | **Substantially reduced.** Use the "Alumni" and "Alumni – Management Library" filters on the A–Z list. Hoovers is noted as available to alumni including Owen alumni. Do not assume PitchBook, Bloomberg, FactSet, or LSEG carry over. |

**Off-campus:** access runs through the **library proxy server** with VUnetID authentication. The critical detail: you must enter via a *library-website link* (Research Guides, catalog, A–Z list) for proxy routing to apply. A bookmarked vendor URL will not recognize the user as a VU affiliate. See `access-troubleshooting.md`.

**Vanderbilt VPN** extends off-campus access to essentially the whole database list. Recommend it by default to any remote user.

**Bloomberg Terminal is the only genuine on-site restriction.** It's a physical licensed seat — installed software on designated machines, not a web resource behind an IP check. VPN does not substitute for sitting at the terminal. Everything else in this reference can be reached off-site by an eligible user.

**FactSet is available off-site** for eligible users, despite guide language that reads as machine-bound ("from computers in the Walker Management Library" describes where walk-up access exists, not the limit of access). Eligibility is entitlement-based — confirm the user's affiliation qualifies rather than assuming. Owen students/faculty/staff are the safe case; if a user hits a wall, that's an entitlement question for the librarians, not a location problem.

Practical phrasing for plans: "All of this works remotely over the Vanderbilt VPN except Bloomberg, which needs an on-campus terminal — plan a trip for that piece."

**Genuinely on-site only:**
- **Bloomberg Terminal** — Walker Management Library computers, Calhoun 215, 4th floor Central (dual-monitor machine). Physical licensed seat; a campus trip is required, full stop. This is the only such resource in this reference.

## 7. ESG / Sustainability

There is **no single dedicated ESG database** at Vanderbilt. ESG data comes through three financial platforms — **FactSet, LSEG Workspace, and Bloomberg** — each with different sources, methodologies, and coverage.

**Triangulate across all three. Do not treat any one as authoritative.**

This is not a hedge; it's the substance of ESG research. Providers disagree because ESG ratings are constructed, not measured: they differ on which issues are material to a sector, how unreported data is estimated, whether scores are peer-relative or absolute, and how controversies are weighted. Two providers routinely rate the same company very differently. A plan that cites one score as fact is producing a weaker answer than one that reports the spread and explains it.

| Source | Link | ESG notes | Access friction |
|---|---|---|---|
| FactSet | /factset ✅ | ESG data and analytics integrated with company financials; aggregates third-party ESG providers | **Remote-capable over VPN** for eligible users |
| LSEG Workspace | /refinitiv ✅ | LSEG (formerly Refinitiv) ESG scores — one of the widest-coverage datasets, built on reported company disclosure with long history | **Remote-capable over VPN.** Personal account, resets each semester; 150 pp/day |
| Bloomberg Terminal | /bloombergmgt ✅ | Bloomberg ESG disclosure scores and data functions; strong on disclosure completeness | **On-campus terminal only — VPN does not help** |

> **Access note.** LSEG and FactSet are both reachable remotely over the **Vanderbilt VPN** for eligible users, so two of the three providers are available off-site — enough for a real cross-provider comparison. **Bloomberg requires an on-campus terminal**, so adding the third provider means a campus trip. If the user can't make one, run the LSEG/FactSet comparison and note that Bloomberg's disclosure-completeness angle is missing.

**How to actually triangulate:**

1. Pull the score for the same entity from each platform, recording **provider, score, scale, and as-of date** — scales are not comparable without normalization
2. Compare at the **pillar level** (E, S, G separately), not just the headline score; disagreement usually concentrates in one pillar and that's the finding
3. Identify *why* they diverge — different materiality weighting, disclosure vs. estimated data, peer group definition
4. **Report the range and the reason**, not an average. Averaging away provider disagreement destroys the most interesting result.

**Supporting sources** (usable remotely, and often what the question is really after):

- **/secreports ✅** — 10-K risk factors, proxy statements, and company sustainability reports: the underlying disclosure the ratings are built from
- **Nexis Uni /nexisuni ✅** — controversy, litigation, and regulatory screening. Frequently the real substance behind an ESG question, and it's the one ratings lag on
- **Business Source Complete /businesssourcecomplete ✅** — scholarly and trade literature on CSR and sustainability, including critical work on ESG rating divergence itself
